Decision Tree · Drug Classification

The Gini Index is a probability experiment

Not a formula. Not a loss function. A question: if you randomly pick two patients from this node, how likely are they to be prescribed different drugs?

Explore the intuition ↓ Jump to tree visualizer
The Core Idea

Impurity as disagreement

Imagine a bag containing 100 patient records. Reach in twice and pull out two patients at random. How often will they disagree on which drug they need?

A pure node means every patient gets the same drug — agreement is guaranteed.
An impure node means patients disagree — two picks will likely yield different classes.

The Formula

Why 1 − Σpᵢ²?

The formula is a shorthand for: "1 minus the probability both picks agree." Here's why each piece exists.

Derivation
Calculation
Python
1
Pick patient 1 — observe their drug class
Say the node has 50% DrugY. The probability the first patient is DrugY is 0.5.
2
Pick patient 2 — what's the probability they match?
For large N, sampling is approximately independent. P(both DrugY) = P(Y)² = 0.5² = 0.25.
3
Sum over all classes — total agreement probability
Σ pᵢ² gives the total probability that two random draws agree on any class. This is the purity signal.
Σ pᵢ² = P(Y)² + P(X)² + P(A)² + P(B)² + P(C)²
4
Subtract from 1 — flip to impurity
We want impurity, not purity. So: Gini = 1 − Σ pᵢ². This is the probability two random picks disagree.
Gini = 1 − Σ pᵢ²    ← probability of mismatch

import numpy as np # Class counts at a node counts = np.array([71, 44, 18, 14, 13]) total = counts.sum() # Proportions p = counts / total # Pᵢ² for each class (probability two picks agree on this class) pi2 = p ** 2 print(f"Each pᵢ²: {pi2.round(4)}") # Gini index (probability two picks DISAGREE) gini = 1 - pi2.sum() print(f"Gini = 1 − {pi2.sum():.4f} = {gini:.4f}") # Verify with sklearn from sklearn.tree import DecisionTreeClassifier clf = DecisionTreeClassifier(criterion='gini') # clf.fit(X_train, y_train) → clf.tree_.impurity[node_id]
